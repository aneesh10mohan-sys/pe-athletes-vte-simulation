Athlete Blood Clot Risk Simulator

Estimates the chance of developing a blood clot (deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism) over the next 12 months, based on how you train. It uses the simulation model from Pulmonary Embolism in Athletes: Evaluating the Thrombotic Risks of Intense Exercise (A. Mohan).

Research model, not a medical test.Some inputs are fitted to published studies, but others are still assumptions, and the model has not been checked against real patient outcomes. Do not use it to diagnose or make treatment decisions.
Symptoms now? Get care now.Leg swelling or pain, sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, or coughing up blood need emergency care. Call your local emergency number.

What would lower it

    What the number means. It is the model's estimate of the chance of developing a first clot within a year. It is not the chance that you have a clot now. Most of the estimate comes from age, weight, genes and hormones. Training adds or removes a part of it through four routes: dehydration thickening the blood, clotting activated by hard efforts, sitting still afterward, and small injuries to vein walls from repetitive movement.

    How it is calculated. Your year is simulated workout by workout, then repeated 400 times with different values for the uncertain inputs. The large number uses the model's central assumptions. The range covers 90% of the repeats. The full method, inputs and their sources are in the model write-up.

    Model version 2 (November 2026): clotting timing and effect size are fitted to published marathon blood-test studies, and fitness protection is calibrated to 14 population cohort studies. The vein-wall microtrauma effect is still an assumption. Details are in the model addendum.
